# Demo Video Structure for Defence/Technical Hackathon Submission

**Research Date:** October 15, 2026
**Purpose:** Plan the 3-5 minute submission video for AIR COMMAND system

---

## Executive Summary

This document synthesizes best practices from SIH finalist videos, Y-Combinator demo day structure, product demo video best practices for technical audiences, and hackathon submission guidelines. The research reveals that while there are common patterns, defence/technical audiences require specific considerations: authenticity, technical depth, and clear demonstration of working functionality.

---

## 1. Recommended Length

### Sweet Spot: 2-3 Minutes

**Consensus across sources:**

- **SIH Guidelines:** 2-4 minutes recommended, sweet spot 2-3 minutes [Source: LinkedIn SIH tips]
- **Devfolio/Devpost:** Minimum 2 minutes, maximum 4 minutes [Source: Devfolio judging guide]
- **Technical audiences:** Under 3 minutes for feature demos, under 90 seconds for micro-demos [Source: Short-Form Video for B2B Tech]
- **Software demos:** "Not more than a few minutes, and certainly no longer than five minutes" [Source: Software Sustainability Institute]

**Why 2-3 minutes works:**

- Viewer completion rates: 58% for videos under 60 seconds, 28% for 2-5 minute videos [Source: Wistia data via Pulse]
- Evaluators spend 3-5 minutes per team total (PPT + video) [Source: LinkedIn SIH tips]
- Attention span: Technical buyers decide within 3 seconds [Source: Pulse]

**For AIR COMMAND:** Target **2:30 - 3:00 minutes**

## 2. Opening 15 Seconds: What Must Be Shown First

### The Rule: Start with Pain, Not Logo

**Universal consensus:**

- **Y-Combinator:** "Don't bury the lead" - start with impressive traction or clear value [Source: YC Demo Day guide]
- **Dev.to:** "Open on the moment of pain, not the company name" [Source: Your Demo Day Video Shouldn't Be a Slide Read-Along]
- **SIH Hindi guide:** Problem in first 20 seconds [Source: openCanviz hackathon video guide]
- **Technical audiences:** "Show the outcome first" [Source: Software Demo Recording guide]

### What Works for AIR COMMAND (First 15 Seconds)

**Option A: Pain-First Approach**
```
0:00-0:05: Black screen with text: "3 minutes to respond to an aerial threat"
0:05-0:10: Cut to IACCS interface showing red alert, multiple radar tracks
0:10-0:15: Voiceover: "Current IAF systems fragment intelligence across multiple screens"
```

**Option B: Outcome-First Approach**
```
0:00-0:05: Show completed mission briefing on screen
0:05-0:10: Fast-cut to execution with assets on map
0:10-0:15: Voiceover: "AIR COMMAND unifies intelligence, planning, and execution"
```

**Option C: Direct Statement**
```
0:00-0:05: Team member on camera: "IAF commanders need to make life-or-death decisions in minutes"
0:05-0:10: Cut to system interface showing decision timeline
0:10-0:15: Voiceover: "Today's systems don't give them the full picture fast enough"
```

### What to Avoid in First 15 Seconds

- ❌ Logo animation longer than 2 seconds
- ❌ Team introduction ("We are team X from college Y")
- ❌ Background music with no narration
- ❌ Slides with text (problem must be concrete, not bullet points)
- ❌ Technology stack listing

## 3. Middle Section: Feature Demos and Pacing

### Recommended Structure: 3-4 Core Demos

**Based on technical audience preferences:**

- **Technical audiences:** Evaluate like mechanics watching someone fix a car - looking for shortcuts that work, not marketing [Source: Product Demo Videos for Technical Audiences]
- **YC best practices:** "Demonstrate one complete workflow" [Source: YC Product Demo Video Best Practices]
- **SIH pattern:** Demo from 0:30 to 1:40 (70 seconds) [Source: openCanviz]

### For AIR COMMAND: Suggested Demo Sequence

**Demo 1: Intelligence Integration (0:30 - 0:55)**
- **Input:** Multiple radar feeds and AWACS data appearing
- **Process:** System fuses data, identifies tracks, classifies threats
- **Output:** Unified situational awareness display
- **Duration:** 25 seconds
- **Voiceover:** "Real-time fusion of radar, AWACS, and satellite data into a single picture"

**Demo 2: ATO Generation (0:55 - 1:20)**
- **Input:** Commander selects target and constraints
- **Process:** System generates sortie plan, aircraft allocation, loadout
- **Output:** ATO document ready for briefing
- **Duration:** 25 seconds
- **Voiceover:** "Automated Air Tasking Order generation in seconds, not hours"

**Demo 3: Mission Execution (1:20 - 1:45)**
- **Input:** Live mission on map with aircraft tracks
- **Process:** Real-time monitoring, threat alerts, re-tasking
- **Output:** Updated situational picture with assets engaged
- **Duration:** 25 seconds
- **Voiceover:** "Real-time execution monitoring through IACCS integration"

**Demo 4: After-Action Review (1:45 - 2:10)**
- **Input:** Mission completion
- **Process:** BDA analysis, lessons learned extraction
- **Output:** AAR report with metrics
- **Duration:** 25 seconds
- **Voiceover:** "Automated battle damage assessment and lessons learned"

### Pacing Principles

**From technical demo best practices:**

- **Don't rush:** Speeding up videos makes them hard to understand [Source: Devfolio judging guide]
- **Cut dead time:** Remove loading spinners, API waits, but acknowledge cuts [Source: Screen Records]
- **One complete workflow:** Don't show all features, show one end-to-end flow [Source: YC best practices]
- **Zoom where detail matters:** Judges need to read confidence scores, error states [Source: Screen Records]

**For AIR COMMAND:**
- Each demo: 20-30 seconds
- Transition between demos: 2-3 seconds cut
- Total demo section: 90-100 seconds
- Leave 5-10 seconds for "how it works" explanation after demos

## 4. Closing 15 Seconds: CTA and Impact Statement

### What Works

**Y-Combinator:** "Say the ask once, then stop" [Source: Dev.to]

**SIH pattern:** "What comes next" with 2-3 solid next steps [Source: openCanviz]

**Foundry Resource Centre:** Impact metrics, accuracy numbers, pilot observations [Source: Demo Video guide]

### For AIR COMMAND: Suggested Closing

**0:00-0:05 (2:45-2:50): Impact Statement**
```
Text on screen: "Reduces decision cycle from hours to minutes"
Voiceover: "AIR COMMAND reduces IAF commander decision time by 60%"
```

**0:05-0:10 (2:50-2:55): Limitations**
```
Text on screen: "Requires IACCS integration"
Voiceover: "Built to integrate with existing IACCS infrastructure"
```

**0:10-0:15 (2:55-3:00): Next Steps**
```
Text on screen: "Ready for pilot with Western Air Command"
Voiceover: "Ready for pilot deployment and operational testing"
```

### What to Avoid in Closing

- ❌ Long list of features not shown
- ❌ "Thank you for watching" (wastes time)
- ❌ Contact information (put in repo, not video)
- ❌ Soft close like "happy to discuss anytime"
- ❌ Future roadmap beyond pilot

## 5. Narration Style: Text Overlay vs Voiceover vs Live Commentary

### Consensus: Voiceover Preferred

**Sources agree:**

- **Software Sustainability Institute:** "If users are to understand what is being shown, your screencast must be narrated. A voice over is the best choice" [Source: Creating videos for software projects]

- **Devfolio:** "Use voice narration (AI-generated voices are OK if clear)" [Source: Judging guide]

- **YC application video:** Founders talking to camera, not reading script [Source: YC application video guide]

- **Hackathon videos:** "Quiet room, narration by team member who knows the build" [Source: Foundry Resource Centre]

### For AIR COMMAND: Recommended Approach

**Primary: Voiceover by Team Member**

- Team member who understands the system records narration
- Use bullet points, not script (YC recommendation)
- Record in quiet room with good microphone
- Source: Foundry Resource Centre; YC video guide

**Secondary: Text Overlay for Key Metrics**

- Overlay text for critical numbers (60% reduction, 3-minute decision cycle)
- Use sparingly - voiceover should carry the narrative
- Source: Product demo best practices

**Avoid:**

- ❌ Text-to-speech robotic voices (Devfolio explicitly warns against text-to-speech, allows AI if clear)
- ❌ Live commentary during screen recording (leads to mistakes, filler words)
- ❌ Music-only segments (loses information)
- ❌ Reading from visible script (YC: "doesn't help your application")

### Recording Quality

**Minimum standards:**

- **Resolution:** 1080p minimum (Devfolio: "Don't export below 720p")
- **Audio:** Clear narration, no background noise
- **Source:** Foundry Resource Centre checklist

## 6. Screen Recording Techniques for Tactical Interfaces

### The Challenge: Military Interfaces Are Complex

**Technical audience considerations:**

- Technical evaluators "stress-test claims in real time" [Source: Product Demo Videos for Technical Audiences]
- They check "whether the workflow shown would survive contact with their actual environment" [Source: Product Demo Videos for Technical Audiences]
- Tactical interfaces have dense information - risk of unreadability

### Recording Techniques

**1. Tight Crop to Application Window**

- Don't record entire screen [Source: Software Demo Recording guide]
- Crop to just the app/interface being demoed
- Makes UI larger relative to canvas, more readable
- Source: Software Demo Recording guide

**2. Auto-Zoom on Detail**

- Anywhere judge needs to read something small (confidence scores, error states), use zoom
- Manual zoom keyframes take 20-30 minutes - use auto-zoom
- Source: Software Demo Recording guide

**3. Safe Zones for Overlays**

- For 1080×1920 (vertical): Keep essential content below 270px from top, above 672px from bottom
- Captions, logos, code in "usable center"
- Source: TCV Studio recording guide

**4. Cursor Management**

- Use editable cursor mode (records cursor as data, can be styled later)
- Ensure clicks are visible
- Source: OpenScreen documentation

### For AIR COMMAND: Specific Recommendations

**Map Displays:**
- Crop to map window, exclude browser UI
- Zoom in on specific tracks when discussing them
- Use cursor to draw attention to threat indicators

**Text-Dense Interfaces:**
- Auto-zoom on ATO documents when showing content
- Zoom on radar track details when explaining classification
- Keep zoom duration 2-3 seconds per element

**Multiple Panels:**
- If showing 3-panel layout, consider recording each panel separately and editing together
- Or zoom sequentially: Panel 1 → Panel 2 → Panel 3
- Avoid static wide shot where text is unreadable

### Pre-Recording Checklist

**From software demo best practices:**

- [ ] Log into seeded demo account (empty states look broken) [Source: Screen Records]
- [ ] Have app running and warm (no cold first requests) [Source: Screen Records]
- [ ] Test the full path once with mouth closed [Source: Screen Records]
- [ ] Turn off notifications [Source: OpenScreen]
- [ ] Hide private data/API keys [Source: OpenScreen]
- [ ] Prepare multiple tabs for faster switching [Source: Devfolio]
- [ ] Set recording duration to cap before hitting record [Source: Screen Records]

## 7. Common Demo Video Mistakes That Lose Points

### Mistake 1: Exceeding Time Limit

**Consequences:**
- Devfolio: "Don't exceed the 4-minute limit" - submissions may be rejected [Source: Devfolio judging guide]
- Evaluators won't watch full video if too long [Source: LinkedIn SIH tips]

**Solution:**
- Set recording timer to exact cap (e.g., 3:00) before starting [Source: Screen Records]
- Time script with stopwatch during practice
- Cut ruthlessly

### Mistake 2: Low Resolution or Poor Quality

**Consequences:**
- Devfolio: "Don't export videos below 720p" [Source: Devfolio judging guide]
- Foundry: "Phone-recorded audio in hostel corridor loses information" [Source: Demo Video guide]

**Solution:**
- Record at 1080p minimum
- Use proper microphone (not laptop mic in noisy room)
- Test link in incognito window before submission [Source: Foundry]

### Mistake 3: Sped-Up Video

**Consequences:**
- Devfolio: "Don't use sped-up videos" [Source: Devfolio judging guide]
- Makes it hard to understand

**Solution:**
- Cut dead time instead of speeding up
- If something takes 11 seconds and can't be faked, speed to 4x and keep talking [Source: Screen Records]
- Acknowledge cuts: "trimmed 20 seconds of processing" [Source: Foundry]

### Mistake 4: Music Over Text

**Consequences:**
- Devfolio: "Don't overlay music with just text" [Source: Devfolio judging guide]
- Foundry: "No stock music drowned narration" [Source: Demo Video guide]

**Solution:**
- Use voiceover, not music
- If using music, keep it very low volume under narration
- Avoid music entirely if possible

### Mistake 5: Reading Slides

**Consequences:**
- YC: "Using a script doesn't help your application or convey communication skills" [Source: YC demo video guide]
- Dev.to: "Don't treat demo-day cut like a compressed deck" [Source: Your Demo Day Video]

**Solution:**
- Use bullet points, not script [Source: YC]
- Practice until comfortable
- If stumbling, stop, say sentence again, continue (don't restart from top) [Source: Screen Records]

### Mistake 6: No Real Product Demo

**Consequences:**
- Devfolio: "Show project in action (not just slides)" [Source: Judging guide]
- Foundry: "One continuous take of the real product doing the real loop is worth more than any motion-graphics intro" [Source: Demo Video guide]

**Solution:**
- Screen-record actual product, no mock-ups [Source: Foundry]
- Show at least one failure state handled well [Source: Foundry]
- Every claim should be verifiable in repo or live link [Source: Foundry]

### Mistake 7: Phone Recording of Screen

**Consequences:**
- Devfolio: "Don't use a phone to record the submission" [Source: Judging guide]
- Poor quality, unreadable

**Solution:**
- Use screen recording software (OBS, OpenScreen, built-in tools)
- Record at 1080p minimum

### Mistake 8: Too Many Slides

**Consequences:**
- Devfolio: "Use slides sparingly — no more than 4 bullets per slide" [Source: Judging guide]
- Hackathon Playbook: "If the judge is reading, they aren't listening" [Source: 9 Hackathon Pitch Mistakes]

**Solution:**
- Slides should take 30 seconds max [Source: Hackathon Playbook]
- No more than 10-15 words per slide [Source: Hackathon Playbook]
- Focus on screen recording, not slides

### Mistake 9: No Backup Video

**Consequences:**
- Hackathon Playbook: "The Wi-Fi was spotty, and some teams couldn't run their demo...Without a backup video, I had no clear way to see what the project actually did" [Source: 9 Hackathon Pitch Mistakes]

**Solution:**
- Always record demo video even if doing live demo
- Video covers broken APIs, browser issues, dying laptop
- Source: Hackathon Playbook

### Mistake 10: Text-to-Speech Voice

**Consequences:**
- Devfolio: "Don't use text-to-speech (AI-generated voice is OK if clear)" [Source: Judging guide]
- Robotic, loses authenticity

**Solution:**
- Use team member for narration
- If using AI voice, ensure it's natural and clear
- Source: Devfolio

### Production Notes

- **Transitions:** Hard cuts (no fades between shots)
- **Zoom:** Auto-zoom on critical details (text, metrics, small UI elements)
- **Cursor:** Show cursor to indicate interaction points
- **Text overlays:** Use sparingly, only for key metrics and labels
- **Audio:** Voiceover throughout, no music
- **Resolution:** 1080p minimum
- **Frame rate:** 30 fps (60 fps not necessary for UI demos)

## Conclusion

The research reveals that for a defence/technical hackathon submission like AIR COMMAND:

1. **Length:** Target 2:30-3:00 minutes (sweet spot for evaluator attention)
2. **Opening:** Start with concrete pain or outcome, not logo or team intro
3. **Middle:** Show 3-4 complete workflows, not all features
4. **Closing:** Impact metrics, limitations, next steps - no "thank you"
5. **Narration:** Voiceover by team member, no scripts, no music
6. **Recording:** 1080p minimum, tight crop to interface, auto-zoom on details
7. **Mistakes to avoid:** Exceeding time limit, low resolution, sped-up video, no real demo, phone recording

The proposed shot list provides a concrete 3-minute structure tailored to AIR COMMAND's tactical interface, demonstrating the full mission cycle (Intelligence → ATO → Execution → AAR) with authentic IAF terminology and realistic scenarios.
