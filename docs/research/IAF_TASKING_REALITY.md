# IAF Air Tasking Flow: Reality vs Commander Flow

**Research Date:** October 15, 2026
**Purpose:** Validate Commander Flow prompt against actual IAF mission planning and execution practices

---

## Executive Summary

This document synthesizes findings from IAF doctrine documents, public analysis of IAF operations (Balakot, Kargil), and comparative air force planning processes. The research reveals that while the high-level sequence (Intelligence → ATO → Briefing → Launch → Execution → Debrief) is broadly correct, the IAF's actual practice differs significantly in command structure, decision points, and the role of real-time re-tasking through IACCS.

---

## 1. Actual Sequence of IAF Air Operations

### Documented Sequence from IAF Doctrine

According to the **Basic Doctrine of the Indian Air Force (IAP-2000-12)** and **IAP-2000-22**, the IAF follows the principle of **"Joint Planning, Centralised Command, Distributed Control and Decentralised Execution"** [Source: IAP-2000-22, p. 1-2].

The actual sequence is:

1. **Strategic Direction & Objective Setting**
   - National Security Objectives → National Military Objectives → IAF Objectives
   - Political leadership (Cabinet Committee on Security) provides strategic guidance
   - Source: IAP-2000-22, Chapter 2

2. **Intelligence & Target Development**
   - ISR (Intelligence, Surveillance, Reconnaissance) cycle: Direction → Collection → Processing → Dissemination
   - Target analysis: Military importance, priority of attack, weapons required
   - Source: IAP-2000-22, Chapter 6; Allied Air Command ISR documentation

3. **Air Campaign Planning**
   - Operational-level planning at Air Command level
   - Course of Action (COA) development and analysis
   - Concept of Operations (CONOPS) formulation
   - Source: IAP-2000-22, Chapter 6; "Joint Approach to Warfare" paper

4. **Air Tasking Order (ATO) Generation**
   - Translation of approved COA into executable tasking
   - ATO is a 24-hour execution schedule
   - Prepared by Combat Plans Division of Air Operations Center
   - Source: USAF ATO 101 guide (comparable practice); NATO ATO standards

5. **Mission Briefing**
   - Pre-flight briefing covering situation, target, tactics, ROE, contingencies
   - Conducted at squadron level
   - Source: Operation Garuda mission briefing documentation

6. **Launch & Execution**
   - Orchestration through **Integrated Air Command and Control System (IACCS)**
   - Real-time monitoring by Air Battle Managers (ABMs)
   - Central control through unified air battle management
   - Source: IAP-2000-22, Chapter 7; Operation Sindoor briefing

7. **Combat Assessment**
   - Battle Damage Assessment (BDA)
   - Operational effectiveness evaluation
   - Post-mission analysis
   - Source: USAF Combat Assessment doctrine (comparable); Balakot after-action analysis

8. **Debriefing & Lessons Learned**
   - Post-flight reviews at squadron level
   - Lessons identified and disseminated
   - Source: IAF debriefing practices; Israeli Air Force comparative studies

## 2. Commander Decisions and Decision Points

### Strategic Level (Air Headquarters / AOC-in-C)

**Decision Point 1: Force Employment**
- **When:** After receiving political directive
- **Decision:** Whether to employ air power, at what scale, and for what objectives
- **Information Needed:**
  - Political objectives and constraints
  - Intelligence assessment of threat
  - Available force structure and readiness
  - Legal and diplomatic considerations
- **Source:** Balakot operation - Air Marshal Hari Kumar's account of CCS meeting on Feb 16, 2019

**Decision Point 2: Campaign Design**
- **When:** During campaign planning phase
- **Decision:** Phasing, allocation of forces, sequencing of operations
- **Information Needed:**
  - Target set analysis
  - Enemy capabilities and likely reactions
  - Friendly force capabilities and limitations
  - Risk assessment
- **Source:** IAP-2000-22, Chapter 6; Kargil Operation Safed Sagar planning

### Operational Level (Air Command / AOC)

**Decision Point 3: Target Selection & Prioritization**
- **When:** During target development phase
- **Decision:** Which targets to engage, in what order, with what force
- **Information Needed:**
  - Target intelligence (location, vulnerability, importance)
  - Weapons effectiveness estimates
  - Collateral damage assessment
  - Risk to friendly forces
- **Source:** Balakot target selection - "Balakot camp was the perfect target...no civilians nearby" [Source: ThePrint interview]

**Decision Point 4: ATO Allocation**
- **When:** During ATO generation (typically 24-48 hours prior)
- **Decision:** Specific sorties, aircraft types, loadouts, timing
- **Information Needed:**
  - Aircraft availability and maintenance status
  - Pilot qualification and readiness
  - Weather forecasts
  - Airspace coordination requirements
- **Source:** USAF ATO 101 guide; NATO ATO format documentation

### Tactical Level (Squadron / Mission Commander)

**Decision Point 5: Mission Execution**
- **When:** During mission (real-time)
- **Decision:** Tactics, formations, weapon release, abort decisions
- **Information Needed:**
  - Real-time situational awareness from IACCS
  - AWACS/AEW&C inputs
  - Threat warnings
  - Rules of Engagement (ROE)
- **Source:** IAP-2000-22, Chapter 7; Operation Sindoor IACCS demonstration

**Decision Point 6: Re-tasking (Mid-Mission)**
- **When:** When dynamic targeting opportunities arise
- **Decision:** Divert to new target, change ROE, abort/continue
- **Information Needed:**
  - Updated intelligence from ISR assets
  - Real-time threat picture
  - Fuel and weapons state
  - Authorization from appropriate command level
- **Source:** RAND ISR tasking framework; USAF dynamic targeting doctrine

## 4. Re-tasking in Practice: How It Actually Happens

### IAF Doctrine on Re-tasking

IAF doctrine emphasizes **"decentralised execution"** but with **"centralised command"** through IACCS [Source: IAP-2000-22, p. 1]. Re-tasking occurs through:

1. **IACCS Real-time Updates**
   - IACCS integrates data from all air defence assets (radar, AWACS, AEW&C)
   - Provides unified, real-time operational picture
   - Enables commanders to make early decisions on threat identification and asset redirection
   - Source: Operation Sindoor briefing; Indian Express IACCS explanation

2. **Air Battle Manager (ABM) Coordination**
   - ABMs are designated to assist air commanders
   - Provide real-time guidance to airborne assets
   - Can authorize tactical re-tasking within ROE
   - Source: IAP-2000-22, Chapter 7

3. **Dynamic Targeting Process**
   - For emerging/fleeting targets (exposed for only minutes)
   - ISR assets retasked based on utility function prioritization
   - Requires minimal delay and friction
   - Source: RAND ISR tasking framework

### Balakot Example: Re-tasking Considerations

The Balakot operation demonstrates **deliberate planning** rather than dynamic re-tasking:

- Target selected days in advance based on intelligence
- 12 Mirage 2000 aircraft with specific loadouts
- No mid-mission re-tasking reported
- However, IACCS provided real-time monitoring to National Security Advisor
- Source: NDTV Balakot planning report; ThePrint interview

### Kargil Example: Tactical Adaptation

Operation Safed Sagar showed **tactical re-tasking**:

- Initial profiles ineffective due to Stinger SAM threat
- Tactics modified: withdrew armed helicopters, changed fighter profiles
- This was operational/tactical adjustment, not mid-mission re-tasking
- Source: IAF Kargil official account

## 5. Real Mission Briefing Content

### IAF Mission Briefing Structure

Based on IAF doctrine and comparative practices:

**1. Situation**
- Enemy situation and dispositions
- Friendly forces and air defence picture
- Weather and environmental conditions
- Threat assessment (SAMs, fighters, ADA)
- Source: Operation Garuda mission brief; USAF five-paragraph order

**2. Mission**
- Clear statement of task and purpose (who, what, when, where, why)
- Commander's intent
- Success criteria
- Source: USAF five-paragraph order; IAP-2000-22

**3. Execution**
- Concept of operations
- Tasks to subordinate elements
- Coordinating instructions (frequencies, call signs, IFF codes)
- Rules of Engagement (ROE)
- Source: USAF five-paragraph order; NATO SPINS

**4. Administration/Logistics**
- Fuel requirements
- Armament loadout
- Recovery procedures
- Contingencies (divert fields, emergency procedures)
- Source: USAF ATO content; NATO ATO format

**5. Command and Signal**
- Communication plan
- Chain of command
- Reporting requirements
- Source: USAF five-paragraph order

### Balakot Briefing (Inferred from accounts)

While specific briefing content is not public, accounts indicate:

- Detailed target intelligence (location, layout, no civilians)
- Weapons selection (1,000 kg bombs, six bombs total)
- Flight profiles and routes
- Contingencies for PAF reaction
- Real-time monitoring by NSA and PM
- Source: NDTV Balakot report; ThePrint interview

## 6. Real After-Action Review (AAR) Content

### IAF Debriefing Process

Based on IAF doctrine and comparative air force practices:

**1. Mission Reconstruction**
- What actually happened vs. what was planned
- Timeline of key events
- Source: IAF debriefing practices; Israeli Air Force post-flight reviews

**2. Performance Assessment**
- Objective achievement
- Weapons effectiveness
- Tactics effectiveness
- Source: USAF Combat Assessment; Balakot analysis

**3. Lessons Identified**
- What worked well
- What didn't work
- Systemic issues
- Source: IAF lessons learned process; Kargil lessons

**4. Recommendations**
- Tactical improvements
- Training requirements
- Equipment/procurement needs
- Source: Kargil 10-year retrospective; Balakot analysis

### Balakot AAR (Publicly Available Insights)

From public accounts and analysis:

- Success: Precision strike, no collateral damage, message sent
- Challenges: PAF retaliation next day, MiG-21 loss, probable fratricide of Mi-17
- Lessons: Need for enhanced air defence, better coordination, lessons from PAF response
- Source: IDSA "Kargil to Balakot" paper; ORF Balakot analysis

### Kargil AAR (Documented Lessons)

From official IAF account and retrospectives:

- Target acquisition difficulty in high-altitude terrain
- Initial profiles ineffective, required tactical adaptation
- Need for specialized high-altitude training
- Value of real-time intelligence
- Source: IAF Kargil official account; Carnegie Kargil analysis

## 7. Where Commander Flow Gets It Right

### Accurate Elements

1. **High-level sequence**: Intelligence → Planning → Briefing → Execution → Debrief is correct
2. **ATO concept**: Air Tasking Order as central execution document is accurate
3. **Commander decision points**: The emphasis on commander decisions at multiple levels is correct
4. **Briefing importance**: Mission briefing is indeed critical
5. **Debriefing value**: After-action reviews are essential for learning

### Strengths

- Recognizes centrality of intelligence
- Includes re-tasking (dynamic targeting)
- Emphasizes commander's role
- Includes AAR component

## 8. Where Commander Flow Gets It Wrong or Oversimplifies

### Critical Gaps

**1. IACCS Role Missing**
- **Reality:** IACCS is the backbone of IAF C2, providing real-time SA and enabling central control with decentralized execution
- **Commander Flow:** No mention of IACCS or real-time battle management
- **Impact:** Major omission - IACCS is fundamental to how IAF actually operates
- **Source:** IAP-2000-22, Chapter 7; Operation Sindoor briefing

**2. Command Structure Oversimplified**
- **Reality:** Three-level command structure (AHQ → Air Command → Squadron) with ABMs at tactical level
- **Commander Flow:** Implies single "commander" making decisions
- **Impact:** Doesn't reflect distributed control doctrine
- **Source:** IAP-2000-22, Chapter 7; IAF organization charts

**3. Joint Operations Not Addressed**
- **Reality:** IAF operates in joint environment with Army and Navy; jointness is increasingly important
- **Commander Flow:** Purely air-centric
- **Impact:** Misses growing emphasis on joint operations (e.g., Operation Sindoor tri-services synergy)
- **Source:** CAPS Air Power Journal; Joint Approach to Warfare paper

**4. Political-Military Interface Missing**
- **Reality:** Strategic decisions made by CCS with clear political guidance
- **Commander Flow:** Starts at military level
- **Impact:** Misses critical political dimension of air power employment
- **Source:** Balakot CCS meeting accounts; IAP-2000-22 Chapter 2

**5. ROE Not Explicit**
- **Reality:** Rules of Engagement are central to every decision and briefed explicitly
- **Commander Flow:** Not mentioned
- **Impact:** ROE constraints are critical in real operations
- **Source:** USAF five-paragraph order; NATO SPINS

**6. ISR/Targeting Cycle Complexity Missing**
- **Reality:** Six-step targeting cycle (planning/direction, target development, weaponeering, force application, execution planning, combat assessment)
- **Commander Flow:** Simplified to "Intelligence → ATO"
- **Impact:** Misses complexity of targeting process
- **Source:** USAF Intelligence Targeting Guide; Allied Air Command ISR documentation

**7. Re-tasking Mechanics Oversimplified**
- **Reality:** Re-tasking through IACCS with ABM coordination, within ROE, based on ISR updates
- **Commander Flow:** Implies simple decision to "re-task"
- **Impact:** Doesn't capture the complexity and constraints of dynamic targeting
- **Source:** RAND ISR framework; IAP-2000-22 Chapter 7

**8. Airspace Coordination Missing**
- **Reality:** Complex airspace deconfliction, ACO (Airspace Control Order), SPINS (Special Instructions)
- **Commander Flow:** Not mentioned
- **Impact:** Airspace management is critical to safe operations
- **Source:** NATO ACO/SPINS documentation; USAF airspace control doctrine

## 9. Key Takeaways for Demo Authenticity

### Must-Have Elements for Authenticity

1. **IACCS Integration**: Show real-time situational awareness and battle management
2. **Three-Level Command**: AHQ → Air Command → Squadron with ABMs
3. **ROE Briefing**: Explicit rules of engagement at every decision point
4. **Joint Context**: Mention joint operations with Army/Navy where relevant
5. **Targeting Cycle**: Show the six-step targeting process, not just "intelligence"
6. **Political Guidance**: Reference political objectives and constraints
7. **Airspace Management**: Show airspace deconfliction and coordination

### Terminology to Use

- **IACCS** (Integrated Air Command and Control System)
- **ABM** (Air Battle Manager)
- **ATO** (Air Tasking Order)
- **ACO** (Airspace Control Order)
- **SPINS** (Special Instructions)
- **ROE** (Rules of Engagement)
- **BDA** (Battle Damage Assessment)
- **COA** (Course of Action)
- **CONOPS** (Concept of Operations)
- **OODA Loop** (Observe-Orient-Decide-Act)

### Terminology to Avoid

- "Commander" as singular entity (use "Air Component Commander", "AOC-in-C", "Mission Commander")
- "Re-task" without context (use "dynamic targeting", "retasking", "ATO amendment")
- Simplified intelligence flow (use "ISR cycle", "targeting cycle", "battlespace characterization")

## Conclusion

The Commander Flow prompt captures the high-level sequence correctly but misses critical IAF-specific elements that would be immediately apparent to MoD evaluators:

1. **IACCS** is the centerpiece of IAF C2 - its absence is a major gap
2. **Three-level command structure** with ABMs is fundamental
3. **Joint operations** and **political-military interface** are increasingly important
4. **ROE** and **airspace management** are operational necessities
5. **Targeting cycle complexity** is oversimplified

For authenticity, the demo should explicitly reference IACCS, show the three-level command structure, include ROE briefings, and acknowledge the political context of air power employment.
